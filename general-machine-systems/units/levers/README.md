# Levers — mechanism, failure, and reconstruction

## Why this machine exists
A lever trades force and displacement around a pivot to transmit or amplify torque.

## Energy and information path
Chemical/electrical/human energy becomes force at an input point; rigid-body geometry transmits torque about the fulcrum; output force acts at another distance.

## Mechanism
Static equilibrium gives `F_in*r_in ≈ F_out*r_out` in the ideal case. Real behavior includes beam deformation, friction at the pivot, contact geometry, and dynamic inertia. Mechanical advantage is paid for with greater input travel.

When studying a real example, first draw the boundary and label energy input, useful output, losses, state, control, and interfaces. Then descend into the component that limits behavior instead of cataloguing parts.

## Failure modes
pivot wear, bending, slipping contact, overload, misaligned force, fatigue

A failure is evidence about mechanism. Record symptom → competing hypotheses → measurement → result → revised model. Do not jump from symptom directly to replacement.

## Experiment
Use a ruler as a lever with known masses. Change arm lengths and predict balance. Measure deviation and identify friction/geometry sources.

Before measuring, predict the direction and approximate magnitude of the effect. Record units, uncertainty, and what the experiment cannot establish.

## Reconstruction challenge
Design a lever to lift a specified load with a bounded input force; choose geometry and state safety assumptions.

After studying, hide the reference and redraw the functional block diagram from memory. Your reconstruction passes only if another person could use it to predict behavior under a changed load or failure.
