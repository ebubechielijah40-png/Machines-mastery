# Questions — save your attempt first

Do not open [hints](HINTS.md) or [answers](ANSWERS.md) until you have written a dated attempt and noted your confidence.

1. Recall: what distinguishes a codebook from a physical signal? Give one input and output at each stage.
2. Why: why must a receiver not immediately emit E on seeing a dot?
3. Trace: construct and decode N E with τ = 2. Name the buffer after every event; predict total duration.
4. Counterexample: give two different texts that become identical when all boundaries are removed.
5. Counterfactual: both parties double τ. What happens to decoded text and elapsed duration? What if only the sender doubles it?
6. Failure: changing one gap turns A into ET. Why can successful syntax validation miss this error?
7. Scope: is a 3τ final dark interval necessarily part of a character, a pause, or an end? Which interface rule decides?
8. Loss: can a receiver always recover letter case after the sender converts everything to uppercase? Prove your answer.
9. Cost: E and T have one mark each. Does that make their elapsed times equal? What exactly is your metric?
10. Transfer: invent a two-symbol codebook where every codeword has length two. Can you omit character gaps? List the new assumptions, including how the first symbol and the end are known.
11. Evidence: which part of a decoder test is an observation, and why does it not show that a lamp behaves ideally?
12. Critique: what measurement would you need before choosing timing tolerances for real light signals?
